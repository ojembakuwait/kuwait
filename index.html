This Is Not Theoretical.

Nigerian courts have already ruled on tampered pages.
Twice, at the highest levels.

FRN v. Ugwuowo (2020)

Federal High Court, Abuja

A lawyer was criminally charged for physically replacing pages 7, 3, 21, 23, 29 and 32 inside Supreme Court briefs — across four separate cases. The substitution was possible because nothing about the binding made it evident.

RCN Networks v. GTB

CA/L/888/2014 · Decided 18 June 2025

An entire ₦1 billion judgment was overturned because an execution page was allegedly moved between loan documents. The alteration was detected only through page numbering inconsistencies — the document itself could not testify.

The Distinction Most Firms Miss

Akpan v. Bob (2011) protects your brief from legal quality challenges. It is silent on physical integrity challenges.

If a page is alleged to have been altered, no case law helps you. No seniority helps you. Only the physical document can answer — and a stapled or coil bound document has no answer to give.

The Digital Tollgate Paradox

E-filing did not retire the physical document.
It promoted it.

Since the Lagos Federal High Court made e-filing mandatory, a paradox has quietly taken hold in every litigation practice:

  • 01First service remains strictly physical. The originating processes that open a matter still travel by hand, in paper.
  • 02Every legacy case runs fully manual until final judgment. Years of live matters remain paper matters, end to end.
  • 03E-filing reduced document volume — and concentrated all remaining risk on the highest stakes physical documents that survive it.
E-filing handles the digital. CDIS handles the physical documents e-filing cannot touch — and those are the ones that matter most.

Regulatory Context

The fewer physical documents a firm produces, the more each one carries. The originating summons. The execution page. The certified true copy on which an appeal turns.

Volume went down. Stakes per document went up. That is the paradox — and it is precisely where integrity infrastructure belongs.
